3. Flash check: which exposure makes the robots' lights stand out

Put the robots on the board, switched on and standing still. The check makes every robot light up white for 1.5 s, four times, while the overhead camera takes pictures at four exposure settings: its own automatic value, then 1/2, 1/4 and 1/8 of it (about 25 s in all). A shorter exposure darkens the board but not the lights, so the lights stand out more; too short and the dark robot tops fade into a dark board, which the tracker needs to see.

This is a small design of experiment: one factor (exposure) at four levels, one run each, three robots as repeats. The levels (halving steps) and the rule for recommending one (every robot's top must differ from the board by at least 32 grey levels, twice the tracker's own threshold of 16; then the setting whose weakest light stands out most) are chosen, not derived. A second round of finer levels around the winner, and the tracker's lock-on rate on footage at each level, would be the proper follow-up DOE.
